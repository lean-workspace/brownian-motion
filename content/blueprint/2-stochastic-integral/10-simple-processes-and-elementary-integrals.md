**Rings and semi-rings of sets**

## Definition: Semi-ring of sets {#def:semiRingOfSets lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetSemiring"}

A _semi-ring of sets_ $C$ is a family of sets containing $\emptyset$,
  stable by intersection and such that for all $s, t \in C$,
  $s \setminus t$ is equal to a disjoint union of finitely many sets in $C$ .

## Lemma: semiRingOfSets_of_intervals {#lem:semiRingOfSets_of_intervals lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetSemiring.Ioc" uses="def:semiRingOfSets"}

The family of intervals of the form $(s, t]$
  with $s < t$ in a linear order $T$ is a semi-ring of sets.

## Lemma: semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union {#lem:semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetSemiring.disjointOfUnion_props" uses="def:semiRingOfSets"}

Suppose that $C$ is a semi-ring of sets, and let $A_k \in C$, $k \in \{1, ..., n\}$ .
  Then there exists a family of pairwise disjoint sets $B_j \in C$, $j \in \{1, ..., m\}$,
  such that $\bigcup_{k=1}^n A_k = \bigcup_{j=1}^m B_j$ .

## Corollary: semiRingOfSets_subtractUnion {#cor:semiRingOfSets_subtractUnion lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetSemiring.exists_disjoint_finset_diff_eq" uses="def:semiRingOfSets"}

Suppose that $C$ is a semi-ring of sets, and let
  $A_k \in C$, $k \in \{1, ..., n\}$, and $B\in C$ .
  Then there exists a family of pairwise disjoint sets $B_j \in C$, $j \in \{1, ..., m\}$,
  such that
  
$$
B\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^n A_k = \bigcup_{j=1}^m B_j.
$$

### Proof {uses="lem:semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union"}

This is easily done by induction on $n$, thanks to the [semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union](#lem:semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union).

## Definition: Ring of sets {#def:ringOfSets lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetRing"}

A _ring of sets_ $C$ is a family of sets containing $\emptyset$,
  stable by union and set difference (and thus also by intersection).

## Lemma: setRing_of_finiteUnions {#lem:setRing_of_finiteUnions lean="MeasureTheory.IsSetSemiring.isSetRing_supClosure" uses="def:semiRingOfSets, def:ringOfSets"}

If $C$ is a semi-ring of sets,
  then finite unions of elements of $C$ form a ring of sets, $D$ .
  We call $D$ the ring generated by $C$ .

**Simple processes and elementary predictable sets**

**Predictable rectangles and elementary predictable sets**

## Definition: Predictable rectangles {#def:predictableRectangles}

The class of _predictable rectangles_ on $T \times \Omega$ is

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal{R}_T :=
  \big\{(s, t] \times F : s, t \in T,\ s < t,\ F \in \mathcal{F}_s\big\}
  \cup \big\{\{0\} \times F : F \in \mathcal{F}_0\big\}
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: predictableRectangles_isSetSemiring {#lem:predictableRectangles_isSetSemiring uses="def:predictableRectangles"}

The class $\mathcal{R}_T$ of predictable rectangles
 is a semi-ring of sets.

### Proof

Take two elements $A, B \in \mathcal{R}_T$ .
  If only one of $A, B$ is of the form $\{0\} \times F$,
  they are disjoint, so both the intersection and the difference
  are trivially in $\mathcal{R}_T$ .
  If both are of this form, then $A\cap B$, $A\setminus B\in\mathcal{R}_T$
  follows from the fact that $\mathcal{F}_0$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.

  Let then $A=(s, t] \times F$ and $B=(s', t'] \times F'$ .
  We shall understand $(s,t]$ as the empty set if $s \ge t$ .
  Both $F \cap F'$ and $F \setminus F'$ are in $\mathcal{F}_{s \vee s'}$ .
  It is now clear that $A\cap B$ is a predictable rectangle, since
  
$$
A \cap B
  =
  (s \vee s', t \wedge t'] \times (F \cap F').
$$

  Finally, the difference $A \setminus B$
  is a disjoint union of at most three predictable rectangles:
  
$$
(s, s \vee s'] \times F,
  \qquad
  (s \vee s', t \wedge t'] \times (F \setminus F'),
  \qquad
  (t \wedge t', t] \times F.
$$

## Definition: Elementary predictable set {#def:elementaryPredictableSet lean="ProbabilityTheory.ElementaryPredictableSet" uses="def:filtration"}

A set $A \subseteq T \times \Omega$ is an _elementary predictable set_ if it is a finite union of sets of the form ${0} \times B$ for $B \in \mathcal{F}_0$ or of the form $(s, t] \times B$ with $0 \le s < t$ in $T$ and $B \in \mathcal{F}_s$ .

## Lemma: predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets {#lem:predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets uses="def:predictableRectangles, def:elementaryPredictableSet"}

Every elementary predictable set ([Elementary predictable set](#def:elementaryPredictableSet))
  is a finite union of predictable rectangles.

### Proof {uses="def:predictableRectangles, def:elementaryPredictableSet"}

This follows immediately from the definitions.

## Lemma: elementaryPredictableSets_isSetRing_of {#lem:elementaryPredictableSets_isSetRing_of uses="def:elementaryPredictableSet, def:predictableRectangles, def:ringOfSets, def:semiRingOfSets"}

The family of elementary predictable sets ([Elementary predictable set](#def:elementaryPredictableSet))
  is a ring of sets, the one generated by the semi-ring of predictable rectangles.

### Proof {uses="lem:predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets, lem:setRing_of_finiteUnions"}

Since elementary predictable sets are finite unions of predictable rectangles
  ([predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets](#lem:predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets)),
  the claim follows by [setRing_of_finiteUnions](#lem:setRing_of_finiteUnions).

## Lemma: elementaryPredictableSets_asDisjointUnions {#lem:elementaryPredictableSets_asDisjointUnions uses="def:elementaryPredictableSet, def:predictableRectangles"}

Any elementary predictable set ([Elementary predictable set](#def:elementaryPredictableSet))
  is a disjoint union of predictable rectangles.

### Proof {uses="lem:predictableRectangles_generate_elementaryPredictableSets, lem:predictableRectangles_isSetSemiring, lem:semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union"}

Since elementary predictable sets are finite unions of predictable rectangles

**Simple processes**

## Definition: Simple process {#def:simpleProcess lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess" uses="def:filtration"}

Let $(s_k < t_k)_{k \in \{1, ..., n\}}$ be points in a linear order $T$ with a bottom element 0.
Let $(\eta_k)_{0 \le k \le n}$ be bounded random variables with values in a normed real vector space $E$ such that $\eta_0$ is $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable and $\eta_k$ is $\mathcal{F}_{s_k}$-measurable for $k \ge 1$ .
Then the simple process for that sequence is the process $V : T \to \Omega \to E$ defined by

$$
\begin{align*}
  V_t = \eta_0 \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t) + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \eta_k \mathbb{1}_{(s_k, t_k]}(t)
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

Let $\mathcal{E}_{T, E}$ be the set of simple processes indexed by $T$ with value in $E$.

## Lemma: simpleProcess_admitsDisjointIntervals {#lem:simpleProcess_admitsDisjointIntervals uses="def:simpleProcess"}

The definition of simple process does not change if we require the intervals $(s_k, t_k]$ to be disjoint.

### Proof

Fix a simple process $V$.
  The union of its time intervals is $A=\bigcup_{k=1}^n A_k$ with $A_k = (s_k, t_k]$ .
  Intervals of this type form a semi-ring of sets
  ([semiRingOfSets_of_intervals](#lem:semiRingOfSets_of_intervals)),
  so we may use the [semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union_finer](#lem:semiRingOfSets_disjoint_of_union_finer)
  to write $A$ as a disjoint union of intervals $B_j=(u_j, v_j]$,
  where for each $j$ and $k$,
  
$$
B_j \cap A_k \in \{\emptyset, B_j\}.
$$

  For each $j$, we then construct the random variable $\xi_j$
  by summing the $\eta_k$'s for which $B_j \subseteq A_k$;
  as $s_k\le u_j$ in that case, $\xi_j$ is $\mathcal{F}_{u_j}$-measurable.
  Each $B_j$ is either disjoint from an $A_k$ or contained in it, which justifies
  
$$
\begin{align*}
    V_t
    &= \eta_0 \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t) + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \eta_k \mathbb{1}_{A_k}(t)
    = \eta_0 \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t) + \sum_{j=1}^{m} \xi_j \mathbb{1}_{B_j}(t)
    \: .
  \end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: elementaryPredictableSet_iff_indicator {#lem:elementaryPredictableSet_iff_indicator lean="ProbabilityTheory.ElementaryPredictableSet.indicator" uses="def:elementaryPredictableSet, def:simpleProcess"}

A set $A \subseteq T \times \Omega$ is an elementary predictable set if and only if the indicator function $\mathbb{1}_A$ is a simple process.

### Proof

Suppose that $A$ is an elementary predictable set;
  by [elementaryPredictableSets_asDisjointUnions](#lem:elementaryPredictableSets_asDisjointUnions),
  $A$ is a disjoint union of predictable rectangles.
  In other words, $\mathbb{1}_A$
  is a sum of indicator functions of predictable rectangles, and thus a simple process.

  In the other direction, suppose $\mathbb{1}_A$ is a simple process,
  
$$
\mathbb{1}_A(t,\cdot) = \eta_0 \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t) + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \eta_k \mathbb{1}_{(s_k, t_k]}(t).
$$

  By [simpleProcess_admitsDisjointIntervals](#lem:simpleProcess_admitsDisjointIntervals),
  we can assume that $(s_k, t_k]$ are pairwise disjoint;
  then each of $\eta_1$, \dots, $\eta_n$ must be an indicator function,
  
$$
\eta_k = \mathbb{1}_{F_k}, \quad\text{with } F_k \in \mathcal{F}_{s_k},
$$

  and $\eta_0 = \mathbb{1}_{F_0}$ for some $F_0 \in \mathcal{F}_0$ .
  This shows that $A$ is a finite union of predictable rectangles, and thus an elementary predictable set.

**Elementary stochastic integral**

## Definition: Elementary stochastic integral {#def:elemStochIntegralBilin lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.integral" uses="def:simpleProcess"}

Let $V \in \mathcal{E}_{T, E}$ be a simple process and let $X$ be a stochastic process with values in a normed space $F$.
Let $B$ be a continuous bilinear map from $E \times F$ to another normed space $G$.
The _general elementary stochastic integral_ process $V \bullet_B X : T \to \Omega \to G$ is defined by

$$
\begin{align*}
  (V \bullet_B X)_t
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, X^t_{t_k} - X^t_{s_k})
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: simpleProcess_map2 {#lem:simpleProcess_map2 lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.map₂" uses="def:elemStochIntegralBilin, def:simpleProcess"}

For $V, W$ two simple processes with values in $E$ and $F$ respectively, the process defined by $B(V, W)_t(\omega) = B(V_t(\omega), W_t(\omega))$ is a simple process.

Note: in Lean, the above is not a lemma but a definition of a simple process with a lemma that its coercion to a function is given by above.

### Proof

It has values $B(\eta_i, \xi_j)$ on the intervals $(s_i, t_i] \cap (u_j, v_j]$ if $V$ and $W$ are defined by the sequences $(s_i, t_i, \eta_i)$ and $(u_j, v_j, \xi_j)$ respectively.

## Definition: elemStochIntegralLinear {#def:elemStochIntegralLinear lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.integralEval" uses="def:elemStochIntegralBilin"}

Let $E$ and $F$ be normed real vector spaces.
We call _linear elementary stochastic integral_ the general elementary stochastic integral of [Elementary stochastic integral](#def:elemStochIntegralBilin) with $V$ being valued in $L(E, F)$-valued and $B(L, x) = L(x)$ the evaluation map.
We denote it by $V \bullet_L X$,
or just $V \bullet X$ when $L$ is clear from the context.

## Definition: elemStochIntegral {#def:elemStochIntegral uses="def:elemStochIntegralBilin"}

We call _scalar elementary stochastic integral_ the general elementary stochastic integral of [Elementary stochastic integral](#def:elemStochIntegralBilin) with $V$ being $\mathbb{R}$-valued and $B(r, x) = r \cdot x$ the scalar multiplication.
We denote it by $V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X$.

### Proof

## Lemma: elemStochIntegralBilin_zero {#lem:elemStochIntegralBilin_zero lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.integral_bot" uses="def:elemStochIntegralBilin"}

$(V \bullet_B X)_0 = 0$ for every simple process $V$ and stochastic process $X$.

### Proof

$$
\begin{align*}
  (V \bullet_B X)_0
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, X^0_{t_k} - X^0_{s_k})
  \\
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, X_{0} - X_{0})
  \\
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, 0)
  \\
  &= 0
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: elemStochIntegralBilin_assoc {#lem:elemStochIntegralBilin_assoc lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.integral_assoc" uses="def:elemStochIntegralBilin, lem:simpleProcess_map2"}

Let $E, F, G, H, I, J$ be normed real vector spaces.
Let $B_1 : E \times H \to I$, $B_2 : F \times G \to H$, $B_3 : E \times F \to J$ and $B_4 : J \times G \to I$ be continuous bilinear maps such that for all $x \in E$, $y \in F$ and $z \in G$, $B_1(x, B_2(y, z)) = B_4(B_3(x, y), z)$.
Then for every simple process $V$ with values in $E$, simple process $W$ with values in $F$, and stochastic process $X$ with values in $G$, we have

$$
\begin{align*}
  V \bullet_{B_1} (W \bullet_{B_2} X)
  &= B_3(V, W) \bullet_{B_4} X
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

### Proof {uses="lem:elemStochIntegral_linear"}

Unfold definitions, use linearity.

## Corollary: elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin {#cor:elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin}

Let $E, F, G$ be normed real vector spaces.
Let $B : E \times F \to G$ be a continuous bilinear map.
Then for every simple process $V$ with values in $\mathbb{R}$, simple process $W$ with values in $E$, and stochastic process $X$ with values in $F$, we have

$$
\begin{align*}
  V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} (W \bullet_{B} X)
  &= (V \cdot W) \bullet_{B} X
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

### Proof {uses="lem:elemStochIntegralBilin_assoc"}

## Lemma: elemStochIntegral_stoppedProcess {#lem:elemStochIntegral_stoppedProcess lean="ProbabilityTheory.SimpleProcess.stoppedProcess_integral" uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:stoppedProcess"}

Let $X$ be a stochastic process, $V$ a simple process and $τ$ a stopping time.
Then

$$
\begin{align*}
  (V \bullet_B X)^τ = V \bullet_B (X^τ)
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

### Proof

$$
\begin{align*}
  (V \bullet_B X)^\tau_t
  &= (V \bullet_B X)_{t \wedge \tau}
  \\
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, X^{t \wedge \tau}_{t_k} - X^{t \wedge \tau}_{s_k})
  \\
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} B (\eta_k, (X^{\tau})^t_{t_k} - (X^{\tau})^t_{s_k})
  \\
  &= (V \bullet_B (X^{\tau}))_t
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: stoppedProcess_eq_elemStochIntegral {#lem:stoppedProcess_eq_elemStochIntegral uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:stoppedProcess"}

Let $X$ be a stochastic process and $τ$ be a stopping time taking finitely many values.
Then $\mathbb{1}_{[0, τ]}$ is a simple process and

$$
\begin{align*}
  X^τ = X_0 + \mathbb{1}_{[0, τ]} \bullet X
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

## Lemma: elemStochIntegral_stoppedIntegrator {#lem:elemStochIntegral_stoppedIntegrator uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:stoppedProcess"}

Let $X$ be a stochastic process,
$V$ a real-valued 
simple process,
and $τ$ a stopping time taking finitely many values.
Then

$$
\begin{align*}
  (V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)^τ = (\mathbb{1}_{[0,τ]}V) \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

### Proof {uses="lem:elemStochIntegral_stoppedProcess, lem:stoppedProcess_eq_elemStochIntegral, lem:elemStochIntegral_linear, lem:elemStochIntegralBilin_const, cor:elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin"}

Replace,
in the formula ([elemStochIntegral_stoppedProcess](#lem:elemStochIntegral_stoppedProcess)):

$$
(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)^τ = V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} (X^τ),
$$

  the $X^\tau$ by $X_0 + \mathbb{1}_{[0, τ]} \bullet X$
  using [stoppedProcess_eq_elemStochIntegral](#lem:stoppedProcess_eq_elemStochIntegral).
Now, $X_0$ may be dropped in the resulting integral,
by linearity ([elemStochIntegral_linear](#lem:elemStochIntegral_linear))
and [elemStochIntegralBilin_const](#lem:elemStochIntegralBilin_const),
that is,

$$
(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)^τ = V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} (\mathbb{1}_{[0, τ]} \bullet X).
$$

Application of [elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin](#cor:elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin)
gives the desired result.

## Lemma: submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg {#lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:Submartingale, def:limitProcess"}

An adapted integrable process $X$ is a submartingale if and only if for every bounded simple process $V$ with values in $\mathbb{R}_+$, $\mathbb{E}[(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty] \ge 0$.

Note that in Lean $V$ comes with the data of a sum representation: by ``values in $\mathbb{R}_+$'' we mean that the random variables in that particular sum representing $V$ are nonnegative.

### Proof {uses="def:Submartingale, lem:tendsto_elemStochIntegral_limitProcess, lem:limitProcess_elemStochIntegral"}

First suppose that $X$ is a submartingale.
The simple process $V$ can be written as $V_t = \eta_0 \mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t) + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \eta_k \mathbb{1}_{(s_k, t_k]}(t)$ for nonnegative $\eta_k$. Then

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty]
  &= \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n} \eta_k (X_{t_k} - X_{s_k})\right]
  \\
  &= \sum_{k=1}^{n} \mathbb{E}[\eta_k (X_{t_k} - X_{s_k})]
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

It suffices to show that each term of the sum is nonnegative.
Since $\eta_k$ is $\mathcal{F}_{s_k}$-measurable and nonnegative, by the submartingale property we have

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[\eta_k (X_{t_k} - X_{s_k})]
  &= \mathbb{E}[\eta_k \mathbb{E}[X_{t_k} - X_{s_k} \mid \mathcal{F}_{s_k}]]
  \ge 0
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

This concludes the proof in one direction.
Suppose now that for every bounded simple process $V$ with values in $\mathbb{R}_+$, $\mathbb{E}[(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty] \ge 0$.
To show that $X$ is a submartingale, let $s < t$ in $T$ and let $A = \{\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] < X_s\} \in \mathcal{F}_s$.
Define the simple process $V$ by $V_r = \mathbb{1}_A \mathbb{1}_{(s, t]}(r)$.
Then

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}_A (X_t - X_s)]
  &= \mathbb{E}[(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty]
  \ge 0
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

But we also have

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}_A (X_t - X_s)]
  &= \mathbb{E}[\min\{\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - X_s, 0\}]
  \le 0
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

Combining both inequalities, we get $\mathbb{E}[\min\{\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - X_s, 0\}] = 0$.
Since $\min\{\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - X_s, 0\}$ is nonpositive and has expectation zero, it is almost surely zero.
Thus $\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] \ge X_s$ almost surely, which concludes the proof.

## Lemma: martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero {#lem:martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:Martingale"}

An adapted integrable process $X$ is a martingale if and only if for every bounded simple process $V$ with values in $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{E}[(V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty] = 0$.

### Proof {uses="lem:tendsto_elemStochIntegral_limitProcess, def:Martingale"}

There might not be an order on the type $E$ of values of $X$, so we cannot just say that $X$ is a martingale if and only if both $X$ and $-X$ are submartingales and conclude with [submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg](#lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg).

## Corollary: Submartingale.integral_elemStochIntegral_le {#cor:Submartingale.integral_elemStochIntegral_le uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:Submartingale"}

Let $X$ be a submartingale and $A$ be an elementary predictable set.
Then for all $t \in T$,

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{1}_A \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_t] \le \mathbb{E}[X_t - X_0]
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

### Proof {uses="lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg"}

Let $A$ be an elementary predictable set and let $t \in T$.

$$
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{1}_A \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_t]
  &= \mathbb{E}[X_t - X_0] - \mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{1}_{A^c} \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_t]
  \le \mathbb{E}[X_t - X_0]
  \: .
\end{align*}
$$

The inequality follows from [submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg](#lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg) applied to the nonnegative bounded simple process $\mathbb{1}_{A^c}$.

## Lemma: Submartingale.elemStochIntegral {#lem:Submartingale.elemStochIntegral uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:Submartingale"}

Let $X$ be a submartingale and $V$ be a nonnegative bounded simple process.
Then the elementary stochastic integral $V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X$ is a submartingale.

### Proof {uses="lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg, lem:elemStochIntegral_assoc"}

We use [submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg](#lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg) and have to show that for every nonnegative bounded simple process $W$, $\mathbb{E}[(W \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} (V \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X))_\infty] \ge 0$.
By [elemStochIntegral_assoc](#lem:elemStochIntegral_assoc), this is $\mathbb{E}[((W \times V) \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} X)_\infty]$.
Since $W \times V$ is a nonnegative bounded simple process, this is nonnegative by [submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg](#lem:submartingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_nonneg) applied to $X$.

## Lemma: Martingale.elemStochIntegral {#lem:Martingale.elemStochIntegral uses="def:elemStochIntegral, def:Martingale"}

Let $X$ be a martingale and $V$ be a bounded simple process.
Then the elementary stochastic integral $V \bullet_B X$ is a martingale.

### Proof {uses="lem:martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero, cor:elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin"}

We use [martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero](#lem:martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero) and have to show that for every bounded simple process $W$, $\mathbb{E}[(W \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} (V \bullet_B X))_\infty] = 0$.
By [elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin](#cor:elemStochIntegral_assoc_real_bilin), this is $\mathbb{E}[((W \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} V) \bullet_B X)_\infty]$.
Since $W \bullet_{\mathbb{R}} V$ is a bounded simple process, this is zero by [martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero](#lem:martingale_iff_integral_elemStochIntegral_eq_zero) applied to $X$.
